Phase-Field Simulation of Sintering and DIGM in Gd2O3-ZrO2 Ceramic Composites

Yeonhak Chu, Yanzhou Ji, Sang-chae Jeon, Kunok Chang

A. Eq. (2) from its definition · B. Why $\Delta G_\mathrm{int}\gt0$

A. Derivation of Eq. (2) from its definition

Notation. Indices $i,j,k,l$ run over $x,y,z$ and repeated indices are summed. Grain $i$ has its cube axes turned by $\theta_i$ about $[001]\parallel z$ (counter-clockwise from the lab axes $x$, $y$), and its elastic energy density is

\[ \begin{gathered} f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta;\boldsymbol\varepsilon)=\tfrac12\,C_{ijkl}(\theta)\,\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl},\qquad \varepsilon_{ij}=\varepsilon^{\mathrm{total}}_{ij}-\varepsilon^0_{ij},\qquad \varepsilon^{\mathrm{total}}_{ij}=\tfrac12\left(\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}+\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i}\right),\\ \boldsymbol\varepsilon^0=\varepsilon^0(c)\,\mathbf I,\qquad \mathbf I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}. \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta;\boldsymbol\varepsilon)=\tfrac12\,C_{ijkl}(\theta)\,\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl},\\ \varepsilon_{ij}=\varepsilon^{\mathrm{total}}_{ij}-\varepsilon^0_{ij},\qquad \varepsilon^{\mathrm{total}}_{ij}=\tfrac12\left(\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}+\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i}\right),\\ \boldsymbol\varepsilon^0=\varepsilon^0(c)\,\mathbf I,\qquad \mathbf I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}. \end{gathered} \]

Shorthands: $\Delta\varepsilon\equiv\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy}$, $\gamma\equiv2\varepsilon_{xy}$ (the same with $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm{total}}$, since $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^0$ is isotropic), $H\equiv C_{11}-C_{12}-2C_{44}$, $\theta_\Delta\equiv\theta_2-\theta_1$, $\theta_\Sigma\equiv\theta_1+\theta_2$. The driving force compares the two grains at the same point, with the same $\boldsymbol\varepsilon$:

\[ \Delta f_{\mathrm{el}}^{1\to2}\equiv f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta_1;\boldsymbol\varepsilon)-f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta_2;\boldsymbol\varepsilon)=\tfrac12\left[C_{ijkl}(\theta_1)-C_{ijkl}(\theta_2)\right]\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta f_{\mathrm{el}}^{1\to2}&\equiv f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta_1;\boldsymbol\varepsilon)-f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta_2;\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\\ &=\tfrac12\left[C_{ijkl}(\theta_1)-C_{ijkl}(\theta_2)\right]\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl} \end{aligned} \]
(D0)

1. Stiffness of a turned cubic grain

A cubic crystal has three constants: at $\theta=0$, $C_{xxxx}=C_{yyyy}=C_{11}$, $C_{xxyy}=C_{12}$, $C_{xyxy}=C_{44}$, $C_{xxxy}=0$. Turning it about $[001]$ mixes $x$ and $y$ only, so every entry with a $z$ index keeps its $\theta=0$ value, and the in-plane entries change through $H$ alone:

\[ \begin{aligned} C_{xxxx}(\theta)&=C_{yyyy}(\theta)=C_{11}-\tfrac H2\sin^22\theta, & C_{xxyy}(\theta)&=C_{12}+\tfrac H2\sin^22\theta,\\ C_{xyxy}(\theta)&=C_{44}+\tfrac H2\sin^22\theta, & C_{xxxy}(\theta)&=-C_{yyxy}(\theta)=\tfrac H4\sin4\theta . \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} C_{xxxx}(\theta)&=C_{yyyy}(\theta)=C_{11}-\tfrac H2\sin^22\theta,\\ C_{xxyy}(\theta)&=C_{12}+\tfrac H2\sin^22\theta,\\ C_{xyxy}(\theta)&=C_{44}+\tfrac H2\sin^22\theta,\\ C_{xxxy}(\theta)&=-C_{yyxy}(\theta)=\tfrac H4\sin4\theta . \end{aligned} \]
(D1)

2. Energy of one grain

Expanding $\tfrac12 C_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl}$ with (D1); all terms with a $z$ index, and the constants $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, $C_{44}$, go into $F$, which does not depend on $\theta$. The $\sin^22\theta$ terms combine to $\tfrac H4\sin^22\theta\left[\gamma^2-(\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy})^2\right]$:

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta)&=\tfrac12\Big[C_{xxxx}\left(\varepsilon_{xx}^2+\varepsilon_{yy}^2\right)+2C_{xxyy}\,\varepsilon_{xx}\varepsilon_{yy}+C_{xyxy}\,\gamma^2\Big]+C_{xxxy}\,\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon+\dots\\ &=F+\frac H4\Big[\gamma\Delta\varepsilon\,\sin4\theta-\left(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\right)\sin^22\theta\Big] \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} f_{\mathrm{el}}(\theta)={}&\tfrac12\Big[C_{xxxx}\left(\varepsilon_{xx}^2+\varepsilon_{yy}^2\right)+2C_{xxyy}\,\varepsilon_{xx}\varepsilon_{yy}\\ &\quad+C_{xyxy}\,\gamma^2\Big]+C_{xxxy}\,\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon+\dots\\ ={}&F+\frac H4\Big[\gamma\Delta\varepsilon\,\sin4\theta-\left(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\right)\sin^22\theta\Big] \end{aligned} \]
(D2)

3. Difference of the two grains

$F$ cancels in (D0). With $\sin4\theta_1-\sin4\theta_2=-2\cos2\theta_\Sigma\sin2\theta_\Delta$ and $\sin^22\theta_1-\sin^22\theta_2=-\sin2\theta_\Sigma\sin2\theta_\Delta$,

\[ \Delta f_{\mathrm{el}}^{1\to2}=\frac H4\sin2\theta_\Delta\Big[\left(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\right)\sin2\theta_\Sigma-2\gamma\Delta\varepsilon\cos2\theta_\Sigma\Big] \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta f_{\mathrm{el}}^{1\to2}={}&\frac H4\sin2\theta_\Delta\Big[\left(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\right)\sin2\theta_\Sigma\\ &-2\gamma\Delta\varepsilon\cos2\theta_\Sigma\Big] \end{aligned} \]
(D3)

4. Turned frame

Turn the axes by $\beta\equiv\theta_\Sigma/2-\pi/8$, that is, $\pi/8$ below the bisector of the two [100] axes (Fig. 2). Then $\sin2\theta_\Sigma=\cos4\beta$ and $-\cos2\theta_\Sigma=\sin4\beta$. In the turned frame the strains are $\Delta\varepsilon'=\Delta\varepsilon\cos2\beta+\gamma\sin2\beta$ and $\gamma'=\gamma\cos2\beta-\Delta\varepsilon\sin2\beta,$ and the bracket in (D3) becomes $\left(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\right)\cos4\beta+2\gamma\Delta\varepsilon\sin4\beta=\Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2$:

\[ \Delta f_{\mathrm{el}}^{1\to2}=\frac H4\sin2\theta_\Delta\left(\Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2\right) \]
(D4)

5. Complex form

Write the strain as $\zeta\equiv\Delta\varepsilon+i\gamma=\rho\,e^{i2\alpha}$, with $\rho^2=\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2$ and $2\alpha=\arctan(\gamma/\Delta\varepsilon)$. Turning the axes by $\beta$ turns $\zeta$ by $-2\beta$: $\zeta'\equiv\Delta\varepsilon'+i\gamma'=\zeta\,e^{-i2\beta}=\rho\,e^{i\psi}$, $\psi=2\alpha-2\beta$ (Fig. 3). With $4\beta=2\theta_\Sigma-\pi/2$,

\[ \Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2=\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta'^2=\rho^2\cos2\psi=\rho^2\cos\left(4\alpha-4\beta\right)=\rho^2\sin2\left(\theta_\Sigma-2\alpha\right) \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2&=\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta'^2=\rho^2\cos2\psi\\ &=\rho^2\cos\left(4\alpha-4\beta\right)=\rho^2\sin2\left(\theta_\Sigma-2\alpha\right) \end{aligned} \]
(D5)

The arctangent fixes $2\alpha$ up to a multiple of $\pi$, which leaves (D5) unchanged. (D5) in (D4):

\[ \boxed{\;\Delta f_{\mathrm{el}}^{1\to2}=\frac{H}{4}\left(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\right)\sin2\theta_\Delta\,\sin2\!\left(\theta_\Sigma-\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\right)\;} \]
\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} \Delta f_{\mathrm{el}}^{1\to2}&=\frac{H}{4}\left(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\right)\sin2\theta_\Delta\\ &\quad\times\sin2\!\left(\theta_\Sigma-\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\right) \end{aligned}} \]
(2)

B. Why $\Delta G_\mathrm{int}^{1\to2}\gt0$: $K(\theta)$ decreases on 0–45°

Interaction-energy ($G_\mathrm{int}$) model [1]: each grain has a constant $K_i=K(\theta_i)$. The local energy difference is poster Eq. (3):

\[ \Delta G_\mathrm{int}^{1\to2}=\big[K(\theta_1)-K(\theta_2)\big]\,4\eta_1^2\eta_2^2\,(c-c_0)^2 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta G_\mathrm{int}^{1\to2}&=\big[K(\theta_1)-K(\theta_2)\big]\\ &\quad\times4\eta_1^2\eta_2^2\,(c-c_0)^2 \end{aligned} \]
(K1)

The second factor is never negative, so the sign of (K1) is the sign of $K(\theta_1)-K(\theta_2)$: it remains to show that $K$ decreases from 0 to 45°.

B1. $K(\theta)=Y(\theta)\,\varepsilon_V^2$

$Y$ is the coherency modulus [2] of a thin Gd-rich layer parallel to the GB (normal $x$). Per unit $(c-c_0)$ the layer carries the Vegard eigenstrain $\varepsilon_V\mathbf I$, is coherent with the grain in the GB plane ($\varepsilon^{\mathrm{total}}_{yy}=\varepsilon^{\mathrm{total}}_{zz}=0$, so $\varepsilon_{yy}=\varepsilon_{zz}=-\varepsilon_V$) and is free along its normal ($\sigma_{xx}=\sigma_{xy}=0$). With the stiffness (D1) of a grain turned by $\theta$, the two stress conditions fix $\varepsilon_{xx}$ and $\gamma$:

\[ C_{xxxx}\,\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{xxxy}\,\gamma=(C_{xxyy}+C_{12})\,\varepsilon_V,\qquad C_{xxxy}\,\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{xyxy}\,\gamma=-C_{xxxy}\,\varepsilon_V \]
\[ \begin{aligned} C_{xxxx}\,\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{xxxy}\,\gamma&=(C_{xxyy}+C_{12})\,\varepsilon_V,\\ C_{xxxy}\,\varepsilon_{xx}+C_{xyxy}\,\gamma&=-C_{xxxy}\,\varepsilon_V \end{aligned} \]
(K2)

and the energy $\tfrac12 C_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl}=\tfrac12(\sigma_{yy}\varepsilon_{yy}+\sigma_{zz}\varepsilon_{zz})=Y\varepsilon_V^2$ depends on $\theta$ only through $s\equiv\sin^22\theta$:

\[ Y(s)=\frac{C_{11}+2C_{12}}{2}\left[3-\frac{(C_{11}+2C_{12})\big(C_{44}+\frac H2 s\big)}{C_{11}C_{44}+\frac H4\big(C_{11}+C_{12}\big)\,s}\right] \]
\[ \begin{aligned} Y(s)&=\frac{C_{11}+2C_{12}}{2}\\ &\quad\times\left[3-\frac{(C_{11}+2C_{12})\big(C_{44}+\frac H2 s\big)}{C_{11}C_{44}+\frac H4\big(C_{11}+C_{12}\big)\,s}\right] \end{aligned} \]
(K3)

At $s=0$ this is Cahn's $Y_{100}=(C_{11}+2C_{12})(C_{11}-C_{12})/C_{11}$ [2].

B2. $K$ decreases on 0–45°, so $\Delta G_\mathrm{int}^{1\to2}\gt0$

\[ \frac{dY}{ds}=-\frac{(C_{11}+2C_{12})^2\,H\,C_{44}\,(C_{11}-C_{12})}{8\big[C_{11}C_{44}+\frac H4(C_{11}+C_{12})\,s\big]^2} \]
(K4)

Every factor except $H$ is positive, so $\operatorname{sign}(dY/ds)=-\operatorname{sign}H$. For this fluorite [3], $H=3288.4-587.8-2\times390.6=1919.4$ eV/nm$^3\gt0$: $Y$, and with it $K$, falls as $s$ grows, and $s=\sin^22\theta$ grows with $|\theta|$ on 0–45°. Therefore

\[ |\theta_1|\lt|\theta_2|\le45^\circ\ \Longrightarrow\ K(\theta_1)\gt K(\theta_2)\ \Longrightarrow\ \Delta G_\mathrm{int}^{1\to2}\gt0 . \]
\[ \begin{gathered} |\theta_1|\lt|\theta_2|\le45^\circ\\ \Longrightarrow\ K(\theta_1)\gt K(\theta_2)\ \Longrightarrow\ \Delta G_\mathrm{int}^{1\to2}\gt0 . \end{gathered} \]
(K5)

In the poster runs $\theta_1=0$ and $\theta_2=10$–$45^\circ$, so grain 2, the one with the larger tilt, grows (Table B1, Fig. B2). For a cubic material with $H\lt0$ the inequality reverses.
