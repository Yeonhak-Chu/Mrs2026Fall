Phase-Field Simulation of Sintering and DIGM in Gd2O3-ZrO2 Ceramic Composites

Yeonhak Chu, Yanzhou Ji, Sang-chae Jeon, Kunok Chang

Supplementary derivation. The elastic driving force for grain-boundary (GB) migration in a cubic [001]-tilt bicrystal: the elastic energy per unit volume released when a point turns from grain 1 into grain 2 at fixed strain.

Result

\[ \boxed{\;\dfel=\left[\frac{C_{11}^f-C_{12}^f-2C_{44}^f}{4}\right]\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\big)\,\big[\sin2\Delta_\theta\big]\left[\sin2\!\left(\Sigma_\theta-\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\right)\right]\;} \]
\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} \dfel&=\left[\frac{C_{11}^f-C_{12}^f-2C_{44}^f}{4}\right]\\ &\quad\times\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\big)\,\big[\sin2\Delta_\theta\big]\\ &\quad\times\left[\sin2\!\left(\Sigma_\theta-\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\right)\right] \end{aligned}} \]
  • $\Delta_\theta\equiv\theta_2-\theta_1$, $\Sigma_\theta\equiv\theta_1+\theta_2$; $\theta_i$ is the angle from the lab $x$ axis to the [100] axis of grain $i$ (counter-clockwise).
  • $\Delta\varepsilon\equiv\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy}$, $\gamma\equiv\gamma_{xy}=2\varepsilon_{xy}$: lab-frame strains, GB normal along $x$.
  • $\dfel>0$: the GB moves into grain 1 (grain 2 grows).
$\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2=\rho^2$
Strain magnitude: $\ge 0$ and the same in every frame.
$\sin2\Delta_\theta$
Misorientation: $>0$ for $0^\circ<\Delta_\theta<90^\circ$; sets the size.
$\sin2\big(\Sigma_\theta-\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\big)$
Sign: orientation of the grain pair against the strain direction.

1.Setup and notation

  • Two-dimensional plane strain. The tilt axis is $z\parallel[001]$; the GB normal is $x$.
  • $\theta_i$: counter-clockwise angle from the lab $x$ axis to the [100] axis of grain $i$.
  • Rotation convention: $a=R_z(\theta)$ is the active rotation from crystal to lab axes; $\theta=0$ gives $a=I$. (In MOOSE this is euler_angle_1 = −θ.)
\begin{align} C_{ijkl}(\theta)&=a_{ip}\,a_{jq}\,a_{kr}\,a_{ls}\,C_{pqrs}(0^\circ), \label{eq:rot}\\ a=R_z(\theta)&=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta&0\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\ 0&0&1\end{pmatrix}. \notag \end{align}
  • Strains: $\varepsilon_m=(\varepsilon_{xx}+\varepsilon_{yy})/2$, $\Delta\varepsilon\equiv\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy}$, $\gamma\equiv\gamma_{xy}=2\varepsilon_{xy}$ (engineering shear), $\varepsilon_{xz}=\varepsilon_{yz}=0$.
  • Anisotropy: $H\equiv C_{11}-C_{12}-2C_{44}$. Fluorite: $C_{11}=3288.4$, $C_{12}=587.8$, $C_{44}=390.6$ eV/nm³ (526.8, 94.2, 62.6 GPa), so $H=1919.4$ eV/nm³ $>0$ and the Zener ratio is $A=2C_{44}/(C_{11}-C_{12})=0.29$.
  • The superscript $f$ (fluorite) is dropped below: $C_{11}\equiv C_{11}^f$, etc. The prefactor of the result is $H/4$.

2.Stiffness of a grain at θ = 0°

At $\theta=0^\circ$ the cube axes lie along $x,y,z$, so the stiffness has the plain cubic form (Voigt order 11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12):

\[ C(0^\circ)=\begin{pmatrix} C_{11}&C_{12}&C_{12}&0&0&0\\ C_{12}&C_{11}&C_{12}&0&0&0\\ C_{12}&C_{12}&C_{11}&0&0&0\\ 0&0&0&C_{44}&0&0\\ 0&0&0&0&C_{44}&0\\ 0&0&0&0&0&C_{44} \end{pmatrix} \]

3.Rotation about [001]: C(θ)

A rotation about [001] changes only the in-plane entries. Each changed entry is its $\theta=0$ value plus or minus $\tfrac H2\sin^2 2\theta$ or $\tfrac H4\sin4\theta$:

\begin{align} C_{1111}=C_{2222}&=C_{11}-\tfrac{H}{2}\sin^2 2\theta, \notag\\ C_{1122}&=C_{12}+\tfrac{H}{2}\sin^2 2\theta, \notag\\ C_{1212}&=C_{44}+\tfrac{H}{2}\sin^2 2\theta, \notag\\ C_{1112}=-C_{2212}&=\tfrac{H}{4}\sin 4\theta, \label{eq:Ctheta} \end{align}

while $C_{1133}=C_{2233}=C_{12}$, $C_{3333}=C_{11}$ and $C_{2323}=C_{1313}=C_{44}$ are unchanged. With $s\equiv\sin^2 2\theta$ and $q\equiv\sin 4\theta$:

\[ C(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix} C_{11}-\frac H2 s&C_{12}+\frac H2 s&C_{12}&0&0&\frac H4 q\\ C_{12}+\frac H2 s&C_{11}-\frac H2 s&C_{12}&0&0&-\frac H4 q\\ C_{12}&C_{12}&C_{11}&0&0&0\\ 0&0&0&C_{44}&0&0\\ 0&0&0&0&C_{44}&0\\ \frac H4 q&-\frac H4 q&0&0&0&C_{44}+\frac H2 s \end{pmatrix} \]
  • From $0^\circ$ to $45^\circ$, $C_{1111}$ falls, $C_{1122}$ and $C_{1212}$ rise, and $C_{1112}$ is non-zero only in between.
  • This closed form matches the stiffness used in the MOOSE runs ($\theta$ = 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45°) to within $2\times10^{-12}$ eV/nm³.

4.Definition of Δfel1→2

\begin{align} \fel(\theta)&=\tfrac12\,\varepsilon_{ij}\,C_{ijkl}(\theta)\,\varepsilon_{kl}, \notag\\ \dfel(\mathbf r)&\equiv \fel\big(\theta_1;\varepsilon(\mathbf r),c(\mathbf r)\big) \notag\\ &\quad-\fel\big(\theta_2;\varepsilon(\mathbf r),c(\mathbf r)\big). \label{eq:def} \end{align}
  • At the same point $\mathbf r$ both grains carry the same strain $\varepsilon$ and Gd concentration $c$; only the stiffness differs (Voigt interpolation, isostrain).
  • Meaning: the elastic energy per unit volume a point would release if it turned from grain 1 into grain 2 while keeping its strain.
  • Sign: $\dfel>0$ moves the GB into grain 1, so grain 2 grows; $\dfel<0$ does the opposite.

Example, $\theta_1=0^\circ$ and $\theta_2=45^\circ$: the two shear stiffnesses swap.

\begin{align} \dfel &=\Big(\tfrac12\,\tfrac{C_{11}-C_{12}}{2}\,\Delta\varepsilon^2+\tfrac12\,C_{44}\,\gamma^2\Big) \notag\\ &\quad-\Big(\tfrac12\,C_{44}\,\Delta\varepsilon^2+\tfrac12\,\tfrac{C_{11}-C_{12}}{2}\,\gamma^2\Big) \notag\\ &=\frac{H}{4}\,\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\big). \label{eq:045} \end{align}

The first bracket is grain 1, the second grain 2. How this local definition relates to the exact (Eshelby) driving force on a sharp interface is summarized in Appendix A.

5.Any θ1, θ2

5.1 Expanding fel(θ)

With $\varepsilon_{xz}=\varepsilon_{yz}=0$ and the symmetries in \eqref{eq:Ctheta},

\[ \begin{aligned} \fel(\theta)&=\tfrac12\Big[C_{1111}\big(\varepsilon_{xx}^2+\varepsilon_{yy}^2\big)+2C_{1122}\,\varepsilon_{xx}\varepsilon_{yy}\\ &\qquad+4C_{1212}\,\varepsilon_{xy}^2+4C_{1112}\,\varepsilon_{xy}\,\Delta\varepsilon\Big]\\ &\quad+2C_{12}\,\varepsilon_m\varepsilon_{zz}+\tfrac12 C_{11}\,\varepsilon_{zz}^2 . \end{aligned} \]

The $\tfrac H2\sin^2 2\theta$ parts of $C_{1111}$, $C_{1122}$, $C_{1212}$ enter with signs $(-,+,+)$ and combine into $-(\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy})^2+(2\varepsilon_{xy})^2=-(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2)$; the $C_{1112}$ term is $2C_{1112}\,\varepsilon_{xy}\Delta\varepsilon=C_{1112}\,\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon$. Hence

\begin{align} \fel(\theta)&=\fel(0^\circ)-\frac H4\sin^2 2\theta\,\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\big) \notag\\ &\quad+\frac H4\sin 4\theta\;\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon, \label{eq:fexp} \end{align} \[ \begin{aligned} \fel(0^\circ)&=(C_{11}+C_{12})\,\varepsilon_m^2+\tfrac14(C_{11}-C_{12})\,\Delta\varepsilon^2\\ &\quad+\tfrac12 C_{44}\,\gamma^2+2C_{12}\,\varepsilon_m\varepsilon_{zz}+\tfrac12 C_{11}\,\varepsilon_{zz}^2 . \end{aligned} \]

5.2 Taking the difference

$\fel(0^\circ)$ does not depend on $\theta$, so it cancels, together with every $(\varepsilon_{xx}+\varepsilon_{yy})^2$ and $\varepsilon_{zz}$ term:

\[ \begin{aligned} \dfel=\frac12\Big[\frac H2\Big]\Big\{&\big[\sin^2 2\theta_2-\sin^2 2\theta_1\big]\\ &\quad\times\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\big)\\ &+\big[\sin 4\theta_1-\sin 4\theta_2\big]\,\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon\Big\}. \end{aligned} \]

5.3 Collecting with trigonometric identities

\[ \begin{gathered} \sin^2 A-\sin^2 B=\sin(A+B)\,\sin(A-B),\\ \sin A-\sin B=2\cos\tfrac{A+B}{2}\,\sin\tfrac{A-B}{2}, \end{gathered} \] \[ \begin{gathered} \sin^2 2\theta_2-\sin^2 2\theta_1=\sin 2\Sigma_\theta\,\sin 2\Delta_\theta,\\ \sin 4\theta_1-\sin 4\theta_2=-2\cos 2\Sigma_\theta\,\sin 2\Delta_\theta . \end{gathered} \] \begin{align} \dfel=\frac H4\sin 2\Delta_\theta\,\Big[&\sin 2\Sigma_\theta\,\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\big) \notag\\ &-2\cos 2\Sigma_\theta\,\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon\Big] \label{eq:general} \end{align}
  • $\theta_1=0^\circ$, $\theta_2=45^\circ$ gives back \eqref{eq:045}.
  • $\theta_1=-\theta_2$ ($\Sigma_\theta=0$) gives $-\tfrac H2\sin 2\Delta_\theta\,\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon$, the same form as the symmetric-boundary expression of Schönfelder et al. (1997), $f(\theta_2)-f(\theta_1)=c_A\sin 2\Delta_\theta\,\varepsilon_{xy}(\varepsilon_{yy}-\varepsilon_{xx})$ with $c_A\equiv 2C_{44}-(C_{11}-C_{12})=-H$.

6.The β-rotated frame

Turning the strain axes by $\beta$ removes the cross term $\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon$:

\[ \beta\equiv\frac{\theta_1+\theta_2}{2}-\frac\pi8 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 4\beta=2\Sigma_\theta-\frac\pi2, \] \[ \sin 2\Sigma_\theta=\cos 4\beta,\qquad -\cos 2\Sigma_\theta=\sin 4\beta . \]

The same strain in axes turned by $+\beta$ (passive rotation):

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta\varepsilon'&=\Delta\varepsilon\cos 2\beta+\gamma\sin 2\beta,\\ \gamma'&=\gamma\cos 2\beta-\Delta\varepsilon\sin 2\beta . \end{aligned} \]

With $\cos^2x-\sin^2x=\cos 2x$ and $2\sin x\cos x=\sin 2x$,

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2&=\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2\big)\cos 4\beta\\ &\quad+2\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon\,\sin 4\beta , \end{aligned} \]

which is exactly the bracket in \eqref{eq:general}. Therefore

\begin{equation} \boxed{\;\dfel=\frac H4\sin 2\Delta_\theta\,\big(\Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2\big)\;} \label{eq:beta} \end{equation}
  • In the $\beta$ frame the sign of $\dfel$ is decided only by comparing $|\Delta\varepsilon'|$ with $|\gamma'|$.
  • $\beta$ is fixed only up to $90^\circ$; using $\beta+45^\circ$ flips the sign of the bracket, so the definition above must be kept.

7.Back to lab-frame strains

7.1 The strain as one complex number

\[ \begin{aligned} \zeta&\equiv\Delta\varepsilon+i\gamma=\rho\,e^{i2\alpha},\\ \rho^2&=\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2,\qquad \tan 2\alpha=\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon} \end{aligned} \]
  • $\rho$ is the diameter of the strain Mohr circle ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm I}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{II}}$) and is the same in every frame.
  • $\alpha$ is the direction of maximum stretch, measured from $x$. The angle is doubled because a strain is unchanged when the axes turn by $180^\circ$.

The two rotation formulas of Section 6 are one complex multiplication:

\[ \begin{aligned} \zeta'&\equiv\Delta\varepsilon'+i\gamma'=\zeta\,e^{-i2\beta}\\ &=(\Delta\varepsilon+i\gamma)(\cos 2\beta-i\sin 2\beta)\\ &=\underbrace{(\Delta\varepsilon\cos 2\beta+\gamma\sin 2\beta)}_{\Delta\varepsilon'}\\ &\quad+i\,\underbrace{(\gamma\cos 2\beta-\Delta\varepsilon\sin 2\beta)}_{\gamma'} \end{aligned} \] \begin{align} \zeta'&=\rho\,e^{i2\alpha}e^{-i2\beta}=\rho\,e^{i\psi}, \notag\\ \psi&\equiv 2\alpha-2\beta, \notag\\ \Rightarrow\quad \Delta\varepsilon'&=\rho\cos\psi,\qquad \gamma'=\rho\sin\psi . \label{eq:psi} \end{align}

7.2 Double-angle formula, then β and α

\begin{align} \Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2 &=\rho^2\cos^2\psi-\rho^2\sin^2\psi \notag\\ &=\rho^2\cos 2\psi \notag\\ &=\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\big)\cos(4\alpha-4\beta). \label{eq:cos} \end{align}

The magnitude $\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2$ is frame-independent and comes out as a prefactor; all orientation dependence sits in the angle $4\alpha-4\beta$. Insert $4\beta=2(\theta_1+\theta_2)-\pi/2$ (the grain pair) and $4\alpha=2\arctan(\gamma/\Delta\varepsilon)$ (the strain direction), and use $\cos(x+\pi/2)=-\sin x$:

\[ \begin{aligned} \cos(4\alpha-4\beta)&=\cos\!\Big(4\alpha-2(\theta_1+\theta_2)+\frac\pi2\Big)\\ &=\sin\!\big(2(\theta_1+\theta_2)-4\alpha\big), \end{aligned} \] \begin{align} \Delta\varepsilon'^2-\gamma'^2&=\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\big) \notag\\ &\quad\times\sin\!\Big(2\Sigma_\theta-2\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\Big). \label{eq:bracket} \end{align}

arctan vs atan2. The polar form needs $2\alpha=\operatorname{atan2}(\gamma,\Delta\varepsilon)$. For $\Delta\varepsilon<0$, $\arctan(\gamma/\Delta\varepsilon)$ differs from $2\alpha$ by $\pi$; only twice this angle enters, so the difference is $2\pi$ and the sine is unchanged. In code, $\operatorname{atan2}(\gamma,\Delta\varepsilon)$ also covers $\Delta\varepsilon=0$.

7.3 Result

Putting \eqref{eq:bracket} into \eqref{eq:beta}:

\[ \boxed{\;\dfel=\left[\frac{C_{11}^f-C_{12}^f-2C_{44}^f}{4}\right]\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\big)\,\big[\sin 2\Delta_\theta\big]\left[\sin 2\!\left(\Sigma_\theta-\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\right)\right]\;} \]
\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} \dfel&=\left[\frac{C_{11}^f-C_{12}^f-2C_{44}^f}{4}\right]\\ &\quad\times\big(\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2\big)\,\big[\sin2\Delta_\theta\big]\\ &\quad\times\left[\sin2\!\left(\Sigma_\theta-\arctan\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\varepsilon}\right)\right] \end{aligned}} \]

In polar form, $\dfel=\tfrac H4\,\rho^2\sin 2\Delta_\theta\,\sin(2\Sigma_\theta-4\alpha)$.

8.Interpretation

  • Three factors. $\rho^2=\Delta\varepsilon^2+\gamma^2$ is the strain magnitude ($\ge0$, frame-independent); $\sin 2\Delta_\theta$ is the misorientation factor ($>0$ for $0^\circ<\Delta_\theta<90^\circ$) and sets the size; $\sin 2(\Sigma_\theta-\arctan(\gamma/\Delta\varepsilon))$ sets the sign by comparing the grain-pair orientation $\Sigma_\theta$ with the strain direction $\alpha$.
  • Physical picture. In crystal axes, $\fel(\theta)=f_{\mathrm{iso}}+\frac H8\rho^2\cos 4(\alpha-\theta)$ (Appendix B). For $H>0$ the energy is lowest at $\theta=\alpha-45^\circ$ (mod $90^\circ$), where an in-plane $\langle110\rangle$ axis, the compliant in-plane direction, points along the maximum stretch. Of the two grains, the one closer to this orientation grows.
  • Reversal. $\dfel=0$ when $2\Sigma_\theta-4\alpha=n\pi$, i.e. $\Sigma_\theta=2\alpha+n\pi/2$.
  • Special cases. $\theta_1=0^\circ$, $\theta_2=45^\circ$: $\tfrac H4(\Delta\varepsilon^2-\gamma^2)$, \eqref{eq:045}. $\Sigma_\theta=0$: $-\tfrac H2\sin 2\Delta_\theta\,\gamma\,\Delta\varepsilon$.
  • Four-fold symmetry. $\theta_2\to\theta_2+90^\circ$ flips both $\sin 2\Delta_\theta$ and the sign factor, so $\dfel$ is unchanged.
  • With simulation output. The $xy$ component of a strain tensor is the tensor shear; insert $\gamma=2\varepsilon_{xy}$.

9.Numerical check

Every form above was compared with the energy computed by rotating the fourth-order fluorite stiffness tensor directly, $C_{ijkl}(\theta)=a\,a\,a\,a\,C(0^\circ)$ and $\fel(\theta_1)-\fel(\theta_2)$, for random strains (standard deviation $3\times10^{-3}$; $|\dfel|$ up to about 0.2 eV/nm³).

Appendices

A. Relation to Eshelby's driving force

Applying Eshelby's energy–momentum tensor $P_{lj}=W\delta_{lj}-\sigma_{ij}u_{i,l}$ (Eshelby 1975, Eqs. 4.1–4.2) to the interface gives the exact driving force:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta f^{1\to2}&=[\![W]\!]-\mathbf t\cdot[\![\partial\mathbf u/\partial\nu]\!]\\ &=\tfrac12\,\varepsilon_1:(C_1-C_2):\varepsilon_2\\ &=\tfrac12\,\bar\varepsilon:(C_1-C_2):\bar\varepsilon\\ &\quad-\tfrac18\,\mathbf b:(C_1-C_2):\mathbf b . \end{aligned} \]
  • Replacing the strains on the two sides, $\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_2$, by one mean strain $\bar\varepsilon$ gives the definition of Section 4. Since $\mathbf b=\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2=O(H)$, the dropped term is $O(H^3)$.
  • Integrating the local driving force of the diffuse-interface model across the interface reproduces the Eshelby jump exactly for a flat interface in mechanical equilibrium with $c$ constant across it. The approximation enters only when the closed form is evaluated with a single strain.

B. Equivalent forms

Crystal-frame decomposition, an orientation-independent part plus a $\cos 4(\alpha-\theta)$ part:

\[ \fel(\theta)=f_{\mathrm{iso}}+\frac H8\,\rho^2\cos 4(\alpha-\theta), \] \[ \begin{aligned} f_{\mathrm{iso}}&=(C_{11}+C_{12})\,\varepsilon_m^2\\ &\quad+\tfrac18(C_{11}-C_{12}+2C_{44})\,\rho^2\\ &\quad+2C_{12}\,\varepsilon_m\varepsilon_{zz}+\tfrac12 C_{11}\,\varepsilon_{zz}^2 . \end{aligned} \]

Complex one-line form: $\dfel=\frac H4\sin 2\Delta_\theta\,\mathrm{Re}\big[i\,\zeta^2e^{-i2\Sigma_\theta}\big]$. Since $i\,e^{-i2\Sigma_\theta}=e^{-i4\beta}$, this is the β form \eqref{eq:beta}.
